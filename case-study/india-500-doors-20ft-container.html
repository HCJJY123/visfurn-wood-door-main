500 Imperial Interior Doors in One 20ft Container: A Flat-Pack Loading Plan for India

A representative India export case showing how flat-packed MDF slabs, mixed widths and pallet sorting supported a 500-door 20GP plan.

IndiaPrepared for B2B door buyers

Direct Answer

The representative India package reached a target of 500 flat-packed interior door leaves in one 20GP by separating door leaves from bulky full sets, standardising protection and confirming the loading plan before production.

Publication check: Representative project — details anonymised. Container count depends on the approved packing dimensions, product construction and carrier allowance.

Key constraints

The challenge

The buyer needed a high door count in one 20GP without compromising edge protection or creating an unmanageable mix of widths at destination.

The specification

The order used flat-packed MDF slabs with a controlled width mix, consistent protective layers and pallet groups sorted by size.

What changed

The buyer selected a slab configuration instead of full door sets, reduced packaging voids and approved a pallet-by-pallet loading diagram.

Logistics and results

The representative plan targeted 500 leaves in one 20GP. The exact count remains subject to final packed dimensions, pallet height, weight and carrier rules.

FAQ

Questions buyers usually ask

Can 500 doors fit in a 20ft container?

It can be possible for flat-packed slab doors with a suitable size mix and packing plan; complete door sets normally load fewer.

Why choose slabs instead of full door sets?

Slabs use less volume and can suit buyers who source frames and hardware locally.

How should mixed widths be sorted?

Use separate pallet lots by width and height, with the packing list and pallet marks matching the approved schedule.

What is the main risk in a high-count container?

Volume utilisation must not compromise edge protection, moisture control or safe unloading.
