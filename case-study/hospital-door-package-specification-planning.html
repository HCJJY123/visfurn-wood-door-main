Hospital door package planning

A hospital door package should be specified by room function and the complete opening. Separate patient rooms, support areas and controlled openings, then review clear passage, frames, vision panels, impact protection, hardware and the documents required for each assembly.

Create a room-zone door schedule

List each door mark with its room function, quantity, wall construction, dimensions, handing and required operation. Separate standard patient-room doors from utility areas, laboratories, imaging rooms and any controlled openings. Similar-looking leaves can require different frames, hardware, glazing or document review.

For each group, identify the requested material and finish, edge details, cleaning exposure, impact protection and vision-panel arrangement. Do not use the single phrase hospital door as the complete specification. The procurement schedule should make the assembly and the responsibility for each component visible.

Check usable clear opening and movement

The leaf width and the usable clear opening are different measurements. Frame stops, hinge geometry, hardware and the opening angle can reduce the passage available to a bed or trolley. Ask the project team for the required clear width and height, then review the complete opening.

For illustration, a 760 mm equipment width and an 850 mm proposed clear opening describe only one dimensional comparison. They do not establish a code-compliant allowance, turning space or safe clinical workflow. Confirm movement, approach space, applicable standards and installation details with the responsible project professionals.

Review hardware and documents as an assembly

Specify closers, pull handles, access-control locks, protection plates, hinges, seals and glazing by door mark. Confirm who supplies each item and which templates are required before machining. For sliding or automated openings, also define operator, control, power and frame responsibilities.

Where fire, smoke, acoustic, radiation or other performance is required, request the applicable documentation and check its scope against the proposed leaf, frame, seals, glazing, hardware and size. A document for one component cannot automatically be applied to another complete configuration. Specialist suitability requires project approval.

Prepare samples, labels and phased site supply

Use a typical opening review to confirm construction, finish, frame interface and hardware preparation. Record the approved revision and the door marks it covers. Changes to glazing, frame, lock or operation should trigger a review of the affected specification rather than an assumption that the old approval still applies.

Group packing and delivery around hospital zones, floors or construction phases. Label separate leaves, frames and hardware so they can be matched to the schedule. Include destination, receiving constraints and target site sequence in the RFQ; production and shipping dates are confirmed after scope and quantity review.

Buyer questions

How should hospital doors be grouped for quotation?

Group them by room function and complete opening configuration. Provide door mark, quantity, dimensions, clear-opening requirement, frame depth, handing, finish, glazing, hardware and the required documents.

Does the leaf width equal the clear opening?

No. Usable clear passage depends on the frame, stops, hardware, hinge arrangement and opening position. Confirm the required clear opening for the equipment, access and project requirements.

Does a hospital door automatically meet fire or isolation requirements?

No. Required performance must be reviewed against applicable documentation for the complete proposed assembly, including the leaf, frame, glazing, seals, hardware and dimensions.

Is this a completed hospital installation?

No. It is a procurement planning framework with illustrative imagery. It does not claim a delivered hospital project, a tested rating or clinical suitability.
