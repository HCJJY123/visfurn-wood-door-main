Liberia Commercial Wooden Door Project

Customer: Alie M Kamara, Monrovia, Liberia September-October 2026

Project Overview

Customer Alie M Kamara from Liberia required a swing door system for a modern commercial complex in Monrovia. The project involved 2,000kg of doors across 22 cubic meters, with delivery to Monrovia Port.

Business Challenges

1. Complex Dimension Specification

The customer's initial opening dimension was 2057×1143mm. Direct production to these specs would cause installation difficulties. Through three days of detailed communication, we adjusted production dimensions to 2040×1130mm, preserving 1-2cm installation margin—avoiding potential returns.

2. Master-Slave Door Color Consistency

Color uniformity between decorative master doors and plain slave doors required material batch coordination and same-line coating processing. Special production workflow was arranged to maintain color difference below industry standard (ΔE<1).

3. Logistics Cost Optimization

Original single shipment quote: $7,700 USD (LCL consolidation). Analysis of the customer's complete procurement plan (doors, air conditioning, refrigeration units) revealed better options.

Solution Implementation

Dimension Specification Standard

  • Door opening dimension vs. production dimension conversion table
  • Hardware placement specifications and orientation
  • Color standard with delta-E allowance
  • Packaging protection options for transit distance

Staged Production Management

  • Week 1: Material procurement and cutting
  • Week 2: Frame assembly and hardware installation
  • Week 3: Surface treatment (anodizing, coating)
  • Week 4: Quality inspection and packaging

Day 16: Production photos and sample confirmation provided; customer approved quality.

Logistics Optimization

By consolidating with the customer's other procurement items in a single 20ft container:

  • Ocean freight: $5,500 USD
  • Port operations: $1,000 USD
  • Total: $6,500 USD vs. $7,700 USD LCL cost
  • Savings: $1,200 USD (18.5% reduction)

Project Results

  • ✓ On-time delivery (Sept 25 production completion, Oct 5 container shipment)
  • ✓ Logistics cost savings: 18.5%
  • ✓ Zero quality complaints (customer site acceptance passed)
  • ✓ Customer satisfaction: 9.5/10

Key Learnings

  1. Standardized dimension expression method is crucial for international collaboration, avoiding 70% of rework risks
  2. Color consistency control must begin at material sourcing stage, not final adjustment
  3. Logistics cost should be optimized within complete procurement planning framework, not in isolation
  4. Regular progress updates and sample confirmation significantly increase customer confidence and satisfaction
