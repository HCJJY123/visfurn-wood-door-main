A Decorative Glazed Door for Deliberate Openings

This French-style suite door uses a solid wood frame with divided glass lites to bring light, sightlines and a residential design language into boutique hotel suites, connecting rooms, serviced apartments and villas. It suits openings where the visual character of the door matters more than corridor-level acoustic separation.

Every lite is specified as 5 mm tempered safety glass. Glass type and thickness should never be left to assumption on a glazed-door order: ask any supplier to confirm both points in writing before production, including on a comparative quotation.

Packaging & Shipping

Packed for Glass, Not Just for the Leaf

A glazed door needs protection for the glass as well as the leaf. Each lite is individually cushioned and corner-blocked to limit movement in transit, with the frame protected separately. Confirm the final packing layout against the selected size and configuration before loading.

  • Individually cushioned and corner-blocked glazing on every leaf
  • EPE foam sheet and corner protectors on the leaf frame, separate from the glass protection
  • Frames, architraves and specified hardware boxed and keyed to the matching leaf reference
  • Approximately 380–420 sets per 40HQ; use about 400 sets as a preliminary reference

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the glass in a French style door safe for a hotel installation?

Every lite is specified as 5 mm tempered safety glass. Ask any supplier to confirm the glass type and thickness in writing before production rather than relying on a generic glazed-door description.

Doesn't a multi-lite glazed door defeat the point of an acoustic guestroom door?

Largely, yes, and this is the trade-off worth being upfront about. Glass transmits sound differently to a solid timber panel, and the grille joints around each lite are additional points where a perimeter seal has to work harder. This door is positioned for suite entrances, connecting doors and villa interiors where the room layout or a corridor buffer already handles acoustic separation, not as a replacement for our flush acoustic guestroom door on a standard corridor-facing room. If sound isolation is the primary requirement for the opening, specify the flush composite door instead and reserve French glazing for openings where the look matters more than the decibels.

Can it be supplied as a double-leaf French door set?

Yes, as a both-active pair with astragal or flush-bolt hardware on the inactive leaf, which is the more common configuration for suite entrances and connecting openings wanting the full French-door look rather than a single leaf with a glazed lite. Confirm which leaf carries the primary lockset and whether the opening needs to close from either side, since that changes the hardware specification, not just the leaf count.

What lite patterns and glazing options are available?

The shown 15-lite grid is a reference configuration. Lite count, division proportions, pattern and glass style can be adjusted against a project drawing; custom configurations usually add approximately 5–7 working days.

How is the glass protected during shipping, and can lites be replaced if one is damaged?

Each leaf ships with the glazing individually cushioned and the lites protected by corner blocking rather than relying on the grille alone to prevent movement in transit, since glazed doors are the item most likely to arrive with a cracked lite if packed like a solid leaf. If a lite is damaged in transit or on site, the glass is designed to be replaced from the grille rather than requiring the whole leaf to be rebuilt, provided the replacement glass is cut to the same specification.

What sizes, finishes and lead times apply?

Confirm the opening sizes, single- or double-leaf configuration and finish against the project schedule before quotation. Standard lead time is approximately 20–25 days; custom lite counts, proportions, patterns or glass styles usually add 5–7 working days.
