Buyer Answer

How should exterior doors be specified for project openings?

Exterior doors should be reviewed by weather exposure, door position, frame profile, seal design, finish durability and hardware preparation. For apartment and villa packages, confirm whether the door is directly exposed to rain or protected by a corridor, canopy or lobby.

  • Exposure level, sealing and threshold requirement

Exterior Door Buying FAQ

What makes exterior doors different from entrance doors?

Exterior doors are more focused on weather exposure, sealing performance and architectural appearance, while entrance doors often prioritize first-entry presentation and security coordination.

Can weather-sealed wood doors be customized?

Yes. Size, finish, frame profile and hardware preparation can be reviewed against the project drawing and finish sample requirements.

What should be sent for a quotation?

Send the door schedule, dimensions, wall thickness, hardware needs, finish direction, quantity and destination port so the exterior door package can be quoted accurately.

Exterior Door Buyer Questions

What exposure details should buyers confirm?

Confirm whether the door is protected by a canopy, exposed to rain, used in coastal humidity, installed in an apartment corridor or used as a direct exterior entry. Exposure changes material, finish and sealing choices.

How should weather sealing be specified?

Review frame fit, threshold, perimeter seal, bottom seal, drainage direction and hardware cut-outs together. A weather-resistant leaf alone cannot solve air, water or dust leakage.

What security details matter for apartment entry doors?

Specify lockset, cylinder, hinge, peephole, anti-pry edge, frame depth and acoustic or smoke-seal requirement. Multi-family projects should also define repeated unit sizes and spare quantity.

What helps an exterior door quote move faster?

Send door schedule, finished wall thickness, opening drawings, finish reference, hardware list, exposure condition, quantity by type and destination port.
