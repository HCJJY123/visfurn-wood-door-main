Hotel Door Package Checklist: Guestroom Doors, Bathroom Doors, Fire Doors and Hardware Coordination

A practical hotel door package checklist for buyers sourcing guestroom doors, bathroom doors, service doors, fire-rated doors, frames, finishes, hardware prep and packing.

Global hotel projectsPrepared for B2B door buyers

Direct Answer

A hotel door package should be specified by room type, door location, opening size, wall thickness, frame, finish, acoustic requirement, fire requirement if applicable, bathroom moisture exposure, hardware preparation, room labels and packing sequence. For renovation, existing frames, hinge positions, lock preparation and door thickness should be checked opening by opening before replacement doors are quoted.

Why hotel door packages need a checklist

Hotel projects often include repeated room types but different door functions. Guestroom entrance doors, bathroom doors, connecting doors, service doors and back-of-house doors may require different cores, finishes, frames and hardware.

Guestroom entrance doors

Guestroom entrance doors usually need a consistent finish, stable construction, accurate frame depth, electronic lock preparation, door closer preparation and acoustic expectations where required by the project.

Bathroom and humid-area doors

Bathroom doors face humidity, cleaning routines and floor water exposure. WPC or other moisture-resistant options may be reviewed depending on design, budget and project conditions.

Fire-rated or service doors

Fire-rated claims must match real project requirements and verified documentation. Buyers should identify which openings require fire performance instead of applying one standard to every door.

Hotel renovation: check existing frames and hardware opening by opening

A replacement door should not be quoted as a generic slab when an operating hotel has existing frames and locksets. Confirm the actual opening before deciding whether the frame can be retained or the opening needs a complete door-set solution.

  • Existing frame condition, clear opening and jamb depth.
  • Leaf thickness, handing, hinge quantity, position and mortise detail.
  • Lock case, latch, backset, electronic lock, viewer and closer preparation.
  • Wall finish changes, thresholds and room-specific fire or acoustic requirements.
  • Room-number labels, replacement sequence and whether rooms remain occupied during work.

Finish consistency and packing sequence

Finish boards, approved samples and room mock-up approval help prevent color mismatch. Packing by room type, floor, opening mark or building zone reduces site confusion.

RFQ checklist

  • Room type and door location
  • Opening size, leaf size and wall thickness
  • Frame, jamb and architrave details
  • Guestroom lock and hardware template
  • Bathroom moisture exposure and threshold notes
  • Fire or acoustic requirements where applicable
  • Finish sample approval and mock-up schedule
  • Existing frame condition, leaf thickness and hinge or lock details for renovation openings
  • Packing labels by room type, floor or opening mark
  • Replacement phase or delivery batch for rooms that remain in service
