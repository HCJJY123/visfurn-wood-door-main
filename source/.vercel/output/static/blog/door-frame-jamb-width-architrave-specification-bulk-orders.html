How to Specify Door Frames, Jamb Width and Architraves for Bulk Interior Door Orders

A technical buying guide for contractors and importers preparing door schedules, wall thickness details, frame profiles and RFQs for bulk interior door orders.

Contractors / Developers / ImportersPrepared for B2B door buyers

Direct Answer

For bulk interior door orders, buyers should specify the finished wall thickness, frame depth, jamb width, architrave coverage, door leaf size, opening direction and hardware preparation before asking for a final quote.

Why frame details affect the whole order

The door leaf is only one part of the opening. If the frame depth, jamb width or architrave profile is wrong, the installation team may face gaps, exposed wall edges, wrong trim coverage or delayed handover.

Confirm finished wall thickness first

Finished wall thickness should include plaster, tile, wall panel or other final surface layers. Do not rely only on structural wall thickness when specifying frames for apartments, hotels or commercial interiors.

Jamb width and frame depth

Jamb width must match the finished opening condition. Some projects use fixed frames, while others need adjustable frames. Buyers should confirm tolerance, installation method and whether the frame is supplied assembled or knock-down.

Architrave and casing coverage

Architraves cover the joint between frame and wall. The width, thickness and profile should be reviewed with the interior design style and site tolerance. Narrow trims can look cleaner but leave less room to cover wall variation.

RFQ checklist for frame packages

  • Door leaf size and thickness
  • Finished wall thickness
  • Frame material and finish
  • Jamb width and frame depth
  • Architrave width, profile and side coverage
  • Hinge, lock and handing requirements
  • Whether frames are assembled or knock-down
  • Packing labels by opening, floor or room
