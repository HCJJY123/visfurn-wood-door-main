WPC Door Frame and Threshold Details for Bathroom Projects

How to plan WPC bathroom door frames, finished wall thickness, tiled returns, thresholds, clearances and hardware before a project RFQ.

WPC bathroom doorsFrame & threshold

Direct Answer

For bathroom projects, specify the WPC door as a full opening system: finished wall thickness, tile build-up, frame depth, threshold, bottom clearance, hardware and packing labels should be confirmed with the leaf before production.

Start with the finished opening, not only the door leaf

A wet-area door set is an opening system. Before selecting a WPC leaf or frame, confirm the finished wall thickness, tile build-up, frame depth, architrave return, threshold position and the room’s hardware needs. These details decide whether the frame and wall finish meet cleanly after installation.

Frame depth, wall thickness and tile finish

Bathroom wall thickness can change once tile, adhesive, waterproofing and board finishes are included. Use the finished dimension in the schedule, then identify which side receives the architrave or return. A small mismatch can leave an uneven reveal or force unplanned site trimming.

Threshold and bottom-clearance decisions

Record the threshold profile, finished-floor level, drainage direction and the required clearance beneath the leaf. The door schedule should also distinguish bathroom, utility, shower-adjacent and dry-room openings when they need different threshold or sealing details.

Hardware must match the opening system

Review the hinge type, privacy lock or latch, handles, fixing method, handing and frame reinforcement with the selected door thickness and frame detail. The goal is not to apply a generic bathroom hardware set, but to confirm the components shown for that opening type before production.

What to include in a WPC bathroom-door RFQ

  • Opening ID, room type, quantity and finished opening size.
  • Wall thickness after tile and finishes, frame depth and architrave requirement.
  • WPC leaf and frame configuration, color reference and surface finish.
  • Threshold, bottom clearance, sealant or transition detail.
  • Hinge, privacy lock, handle, closer and handing requirements.
  • Packing labels that keep wet-area sets distinct from bedroom or corridor doors.
