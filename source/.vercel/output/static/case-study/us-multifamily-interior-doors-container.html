The challenge

The project combined bedroom, bathroom and utility openings with imperial dimensions and a compressed installation programme. The buyer needed predictable packs rather than a generic mixed container.

The specification

The door schedule grouped openings by leaf size, wall thickness, handing and hardware family. Shop drawings were reviewed before production to prevent rough-opening and frame-size confusion.

What changed

The SKU list was consolidated into repeatable door families, pallets were marked by floor and room group, and two shipments were released against the construction schedule.

Logistics and results

The representative route used two 40HQ containers with an approximately 10-week end-to-end site plan. The case should be presented as a planning example, not as an unconditional delivery promise.

FAQ

Questions buyers usually ask

Can a Chinese supplier work from a US imperial door schedule?

Yes. Provide the schedule, rough openings, wall thickness, handing and hardware templates for technical review before production.

How are mixed sizes packed in a 40HQ?

Group each size and door-set configuration into clearly labelled lots and approve the loading plan before booking.

Should prehung doors be shipped assembled?

It depends on the container plan, damage risk, site handling and local installation capability. Compare both configurations.

How do I reduce installation errors?

Freeze the schedule, mark every door set and send illustrated installation information with the shipment.
