Door Size, Frame & Wall Thickness

Clarify the measurements and frame details that affect quotation, installation and final appearance.

The rough opening is the wall opening prepared for installation. The door leaf size refers only to the movable door panel. The frame occupies part of the rough opening, so the three measurements are different.

Finished wall thickness affects frame depth and the way the frame, architrave or trim covers the wall. If frame depth does not match the completed wall build-up, installation and appearance may be affected.

See the manufacturing workflow

At minimum provide opening width, opening height, finished wall thickness, required leaf size if known, opening direction, frame type and quantity. For larger projects, a door schedule is preferred.

Compare material and application requirements before selecting a door category.

04

Hardware & Door Preparation

Confirm the information needed for locks, hinges, electronic locks and access-control preparation.

Lock, hinge, closer, access-control and other hardware preparation can be reviewed when the relevant hardware information and approved door specification are provided.

Hardware should be confirmed before drawings, samples or production details are approved because lock cases, hinges, closers, access control and handles can affect machining and preparation.

Electronic-lock preparation can be reviewed when the hotel lock model, installation template, handing, frame details and project requirements are provided. Compatibility should be confirmed before production.

Provide the access-control hardware model, lock case or template, cable route requirements, handing, door thickness, frame details and any interface requirements that affect preparation.

05

Project Orders

Organize schedules, mixed door types and project labels before production planning.

A door schedule is a structured project document listing the doors required for a building. It may include Door Mark, Location, Quantity, Width, Height, Wall Thickness, Door Type, Frame Type, Finish, Hardware Set, Opening Direction, Rating Requirement and Notes.

A project may contain different door categories, dimensions, finishes, frames and hardware sets. Buyers should organize these differences by door mark or door type in the door schedule.

Prepare a schedule containing each door mark, room or location, quantity, dimensions, wall thickness, finish, frame and hardware requirements.

Project-specific labeling can potentially be arranged depending on confirmed packing and order requirements. Buyers should specify the desired label format before packing.

Packing, CBM & Shipping

Review packed dimensions, shipment volume and destination information before loading is planned.

Export packing should protect the door leaf, frame, finish, corners and hardware from handling damage. The final method depends on door type, project quantity and shipment arrangement.

CBM is calculated from final packed dimensions, not only nominal door leaf dimensions. Frames, trims, hardware cartons and pallet configuration affect total shipment volume.

There is no reliable universal answer because container quantity depends on door dimensions, frame type, leaf thickness, trim, hardware, packing method and pallet configuration. Do not publish a fixed number unless verified against a real packing specification.

Quality Control & Documentation

Align inspection, documentation and rating requirements with the exact door system.

The inspection process depends on the actual product and project specification. Typical checkpoints may include dimensions, surface appearance, finish consistency, frame compatibility, hardware preparation, quantity, labeling and packing condition.

Inspection requirements should be agreed before the order is finalized. Buyers may request documentation, photos, inspection records or third-party inspection arrangements where applicable.

Please send the required certification or test standard for your project. Our team will review whether the requested door configuration can be supported by the relevant available documentation.

Fire, acoustic and other performance requirements depend on the complete tested door system, including the leaf, frame, seals, hardware and installation configuration. Please provide the required rating and applicable standard so the correct configuration and available documentation can be reviewed.

08

OEM / ODM

Prepare the drawings, finishes, hardware, branding and packaging information needed for a custom program.

OEM buyers should provide drawings, dimensions, construction requirements, finish reference, frame details, hardware, branding requirements, packing requirements, expected quantity and project or market requirements where applicable.

A reference image can help communicate design intent, but production still requires dimensions, material or construction, finish, frame, hardware, profile details and quantity.

Customized packaging requirements can be reviewed for OEM or distributor orders. Exact available options must be confirmed for the order.

09

Ordering & Production Preparation

Use this final check before specifications are frozen for mass production.

Confirm the door schedule, dimensions, wall thickness, frame, finish, hardware, handing or opening direction, quantity, project labeling, packing and required documentation.

Common mistakes include confusing rough opening with leaf size, not providing finished wall thickness, confirming hardware too late, not specifying handing, failing to define finish approval, ignoring packing or site-label requirements and assuming certifications apply without checking the exact door system.
