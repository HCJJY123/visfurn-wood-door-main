Smart & Fingerprint Lock Compatibility for Wooden, WPC and Steel Doors

How smart and fingerprint locks integrate with wooden, WPC and steel door leaves — backset, spindle, cutout and power-supply checks before mass production.

Hardware & SecuritySmart lock compatibility

Quick answer

Smart and fingerprint locks can be prepared for wooden, WPC and steel doors when the lock body, backset, spindle position, door thickness, power method and emergency-opening requirement are confirmed for each opening type before machining.

What changes when a mechanical lock becomes a smart lock

A smart lock normally needs more than a standard handle bore. The door leaf may need a different lock-body pocket, spindle or cable route, fixing-hole pattern and reinforcement zone. Confirm the selected lock model before cutting the first sample so that the leaf core and edge construction match the actual hardware.

Compatibility checks before machining

Door systemWhat to confirmTypical coordination risk
Wooden or engineered leafLeaf thickness, core density, lock pocket, spindle and fixing pointsA lock body or through-bolt conflicts with internal construction
WPC door setLeaf thickness, frame detail, reinforcement and privacy or access functionHardware fixing is assumed without confirming the selected system
Steel security doorLock case, cylinder, electrical route, escutcheon and frame strikeLock body, strike plate and anti-pry detail are reviewed separately

Lock cutout, spindle and cable preparation

Send the lock template, side elevation, backset, handle or escutcheon drawing and door handing with the RFQ. If the lock uses a cable between the inside and outside escutcheons, confirm its route and whether the leaf needs a protected pass-through. A typical approved opening is the safest way to verify the preparation before repeated doors are machined.

Hotel and repeated-opening coordination

For hotel, apartment or office packages, keep smart-lock openings separate from standard mechanical-lock openings in the door schedule. The schedule should identify the room type, opening ID, handing, lock model, closer or viewer requirement and packing sequence so site teams do not mix hardware-ready leaves with standard leaves.

What to send in a smart-lock door RFQ

  • Door schedule with opening IDs, leaf sizes, thicknesses and handing.
  • Lock model, template, backset, cylinder or emergency-opening details.
  • Battery, low-voltage or mains-power expectations for the destination.
  • Any closer, viewer, seal, access-control or fire-door coordination notes.
  • One typical opening for sample approval before bulk preparation.
