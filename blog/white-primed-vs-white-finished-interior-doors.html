White primed vs white finished interior doors: what should your RFQ say?

Compare white-primed and white-finished interior doors for bulk buying: construction, frames, finish approval, local painting, packing and quotation scope.

For importers, distributors and project buyersUpdated 7 November 2026

Specify the finish state in words, not just a white photo

A white-primed interior door is prepared for an agreed subsequent finishing process. A white-finished door is supplied with a specified final surface. Both can appear white in a photograph. The quote, approved sample and order schedule must say which route applies to the leaf, frame, trim and edges.

Finish state is separate from construction, core, supply scope and assembly. A primed door is not automatically hollow core; a finished door is not automatically solid timber, prehung or fire rated.

When a distributor should discuss a primed requirement

A distributor with a local finishing operation may want a consistent primed substrate that can be adapted to local colors and stock ranges. First confirm that the selected model can be supplied for that process. Send your preferred dimensions, quantities by size, receiving-frame system and intended topcoat process.

Do not choose this route only because the unit quote appears lower. Allow for local preparation, finishing space, labor, materials, drying or curing requirements specified by the coating supplier, repacking and quality control. These costs belong in the buyer’s comparison even when they are outside the export quote.

Before a batch order, approve a representative substrate and primer sample, then test the intended finishing process with the relevant coating supplier’s instructions. A color chip alone cannot establish coating compatibility or finished-door behavior.

When a project buyer should discuss a finished requirement

A contractor procuring repeated apartment or hotel openings may prefer a defined final finish across leaf, frame and trim. The useful RFQ is more specific than “white door”: state the color reference, acceptable sheen, surface texture, visible edges, quantity by door mark and any existing components that must be matched.

Ask how the approved finish reference will be identified on the quotation and production approval. Where different materials meet, review their appearance together under the intended lighting. Photographs and screen colors are reference tools; physical approval helps resolve differences that images may hide.

Finish approval does not replace hardware coordination. Confirm whether lock and hinge preparation takes place before finishing, which cut edges need sealing or treatment, and what site adjustments are allowed for the selected construction. Do not assume unrestricted trimming.

White finish does not define the leaf construction

Ask for the selected model’s construction description rather than inferring it from a moulded face, painted edge or product title. Record the facing, core or internal construction, edge arrangement and frame material. If a frame is WPC and the leaf is wood based, describe both components separately.

WPC surface treatments also need model-specific review. A painting process suitable for one substrate should not be assumed suitable for another. Use the WPC painting and laminating guide to prepare the questions, then confirm the actual product and surface system.

Neither white finish route establishes a fire or acoustic rating. Where a project requires performance evidence, request documents for the relevant assembly, dimensions, hardware and installation conditions through the documentation review.

Choose assembly and inclusions separately

You can discuss leaf-only supply, leaf plus frame and trim, or a package with specified hardware. Confirm which options are feasible for the chosen model. “White finished full set” still needs an item list: architraves on one or both faces, hinges, latch, handle, seals, machining and assembly are separate decisions.

A knock-down frame package and a prehung assembly can have different packing and installation requirements. Compare their packed size, handling and site assembly implications using the actual proposed configuration. There is no useful universal freight saving to assume without a packing plan.

Use the interior door supply-package comparison and the slab, prehung and full-set guide to put this scope into the RFQ.

A sample approval sequence that resolves the finish decision

  1. Identify the model and substrate. Link the design reference and record the construction that is actually proposed.
  2. State the delivery finish. Write “primed for subsequent finishing” or define the final finish. Apply that instruction to each supplied component.
  3. Review a representative sample. For primed supply, agree a local finishing test. For finished supply, agree final appearance, visible edges and matching components.
  4. Coordinate the working opening. Check frame profile, dimensional basis, handing, preparation and hardware templates where a working sample is needed.
  5. Record approval and exceptions. Identify sample reference, date, approver and revision. Keep unresolved points separate from approved items.

The sample-approval checklist before mass production covers the wider technical review. The commercial trial guide helps distributors and contractors define what a smaller first order is intended to prove.

Build a comparable quotation

Ask each supplier to use the same dimensional basis, construction requirement, finish state, inclusion list, packing scope and delivery term. A leaf-only primer quote and a finished frame-and-hardware quote are different offers, even when both show a white interior door.

For local budgeting, separate the export quotation from your own expected finishing, receiving, storage, installation and destination costs. Request clarification of included services rather than assuming all costs are covered by a single “delivered” figure.

Standard wooden, WPC and PVC batch orders are 50 sets total, with at least 10 sets per model. Sample and smaller commercial trial requests need separate feasibility, price and freight confirmation. No sample credit or future volume commitment is assumed.

Review a design, then send the finish requirement

The white moulded interior door page is a starting design reference. Its imagery does not establish that every construction, primer or factory-finish option is available. Compare the PVC-faced interior door direction if a film-faced alternative is relevant, and use the interior door collection for the current range.

Send your destination, approximate quantity, preferred design, leaf or opening dimensions, frame scope and intended finish route. If a requirement is still open, identify it as a question rather than filling in an assumed specification.
