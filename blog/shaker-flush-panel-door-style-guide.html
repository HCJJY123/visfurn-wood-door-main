Shaker, Flush or Panel: A Door Style Guide for Distributors and Contractors

A visual and technical comparison of shaker, flush and raised-panel door styles for repeat residential and hospitality orders.

Style GuideRepeat project selection

Quick answer

Choose shaker doors when a framed, versatile profile suits the project; flush doors when the brief needs a clean continuous face; and panel doors when the design calls for more visible depth and traditional character—then confirm the construction, finish and repeatability behind that appearance.

Three styles, three different procurement conversations

Shaker, flush and panel doors can all be used in apartments, villas, hotels and distribution ranges, but they create different questions for finish, profile consistency, edge detail and repeated production. The style name alone is not a full specification; the construction and sample should be defined with it.

Style comparison for repeated orders

StyleVisual directionSpecification points
ShakerSimple framed rails and stiles with recessed panel areasProfile depth, rail layout, panel detail, finish and edge consistency
FlushFlat and continuous face for a minimal lookFace material, edge banding, core, groove or inlay detail and sheen
PanelRaised or shaped panel depth for a more traditional expressionPanel construction, routing profile, paint or veneer finish and movement tolerance

Shaker profiles and sample matching

Shaker proportions can change the look of a project door quickly. Confirm rail widths, panel layout, groove depth and finish reference on a full sample. For distributors, this protects the repeatability of the range when later orders use the same model name.

Flush doors and edge details

A flush face may look simple, but the face material, edge treatment and hardware preparation still need to be coordinated. The buyer should decide whether the finished look is painted, PVC laminated, melamine, veneer or another approved direction before the project sample is signed off.

What to include in a style-led RFQ

  • Style reference image or approved sample.
  • Leaf size, thickness, core and frame scope.
  • Profile or panel layout for shaker and panel designs.
  • Face material, colour, sheen and edge detail.
  • Hardware preparation, quantity and packing sequence.
  • Whether the range must match a previous order or local display sample.
