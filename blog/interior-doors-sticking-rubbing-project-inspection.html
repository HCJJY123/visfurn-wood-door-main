Interior Doors Sticking After Installation: A Project Inspection Checklist

Inspect sticking or rubbing interior doors before a batch repair. Record contact points, frame alignment, floor changes and product details for project review.

Quick answer

A sticking interior door is a symptom, not a diagnosis. Before trimming a door or blaming its material, identify where it makes contact, check the installed frame and hardware, and record what changed after installation. On a hotel or apartment project, one documented sample investigation is more useful than making the same unverified repair across every room.

Public homeowner discussions repeatedly describe two different situations: doors that become difficult to close seasonally, and doors that start rubbing after removal or reinstallation. These reports do not prove the cause of a project defect. They do show why a procurement or site team needs a consistent inspection record rather than a material-based assumption.

1. Record the exact symptom before changing anything

Assign the opening a room or door-schedule ID. Photograph the closed door from both sides and the point where it touches. Record whether the symptom occurs throughout movement or only when latching.

ObservationWhat the project team should check next
Contact at the top or side of the frameInstalled clearances, frame alignment, hinge position and the leaf itself
Bottom edge touching the floorFinished-floor level, changes in floor covering and the scheduled undercut
Door closes but does not latchLatch and strike alignment, hardware operation and whether the leaf seats normally
Painted surfaces adhere when closedFinish condition and the applicable finishing or maintenance instructions
A problem appears only at certain timesDated observations of site conditions and any changes since installation

These are investigation routes, not diagnoses. Several causes can produce similar marks, and a single photograph rarely settles responsibility.

2. Inspect the installed opening, not just the door leaf

Check the frame for square and plumb using appropriate site measuring tools. Note where the clearances differ rather than assuming a uniform gap exists. Review hinge seating and fasteners without making an unapproved alteration.

Compare the opening with the approved door schedule and installation details. Was this the correct leaf, frame and hardware combination for that location? Has the frame moved or been disturbed by later work? Were floor finishes installed after the door was fitted?

A useful report records measurements and the method used. “Door does not fit” is much harder to act on than “contact occurs at the upper latch-side corner after the floor finish was completed.”

For bottom-edge issues, use the project's approved dimensions and review our door undercut and finished-floor clearance guide. Do not copy a generic clearance from another manufacturer's product.

3. Establish the installation and storage timeline

Record the delivery date, storage location, installation date and date the issue first appeared. Include relevant wet trades, changes to heating or ventilation, floor-finishing work and any removal or rehanging of the leaf.

Where environmental measurements are available, attach dated readings rather than guesses. Where they are not available, say so. A seasonal complaint may justify further investigation, but it does not by itself prove moisture movement or a manufacturing fault.

Our delivery, storage and site-readiness checklist helps teams collect these records before installation. Manufacturer care guidance also illustrates why storage, environmental conditions and finishing instructions belong in the review. Exact requirements must come from the supplied product's documentation.

4. Do not turn a sample problem into a batch modification

Before planing, sanding, drilling or moving hardware, obtain the applicable written instructions. Ask the supplier what can be adjusted on that specific construction and what evidence is required for a technical review.

A general interior-door adjustment method is not automatically suitable for every WPC, timber, composite or other door. Any trimming allowance, edge treatment or hardware restriction must be checked for the actual model. For a fire-rated or otherwise certified assembly, refer proposed changes to the responsible qualified party and the assembly documentation before work begins.

There is also no sound basis for declaring that every sticking door is warped—or that a particular material can never move. Separate observed facts from proposed explanations.

5. Send a compact, complete issue report

For each affected opening, collect:

  • Project, building, floor and room or opening ID.
  • Product reference, door schedule and available batch identification.
  • Delivery, storage and installation dates.
  • Overall photographs and close-ups of the contact point.
  • Measured clearances and the measurement method.
  • Frame and hardware observations, including any later alterations.
  • Finished-floor status and relevant site-condition records.
  • Number of affected doors compared with the number inspected.
  • Any repair already attempted, with dates and photographs.

Keep an untouched representative example where practicable and agreed with the project team. If a corrective approach is approved, record its result on a sample opening before authorizing repeated work.

6. Decide whether to repair one opening or investigate the batch

Group the observations by door mark, floor, installation crew, date and symptom. Count both affected and inspected openings. A single latch-side contact after one hinge was rehung calls for a different review from the same contact pattern across a repeated door mark. That comparison is a triage step, not proof of the cause.

FindingProject decision before work repeats
One opening differs from otherwise comparable roomsCheck its installed frame, hinge and hardware against the approved schedule; document any site adjustment.
Several openings share a mark or batchPause repeated cutting or drilling; compare untouched examples and ask the supplier for model-specific review.
Contact follows a flooring or fit-out changeCompare as-built floor and threshold details with the approved opening schedule before deciding responsibility.
A fire, smoke or accessibility requirement appliesEscalate to the responsible qualified project party and governing assembly documentation before alteration.

Record who authorizes the sample corrective action, the exact opening used, the result after operation, and the acceptance criterion. Do not release the same action for every opening until the project team accepts that sample and confirms the relevant door construction permits it.

7. Improve the next procurement package

For the next order, agree a sample-installation review and a site-readiness release point. Confirm who checks the finished opening, who approves hardware compatibility and who records the initial operation of the door.

Specify the information that must accompany delivery, including product identification and model-specific installation and care instructions. A clear handover record makes later troubleshooting faster and reduces avoidable disagreement between trades.

Explore interior door options and WPC doors against the requirements of each room type, rather than selecting by appearance alone. For a project discussion, provide the door schedule, quantities, opening details and installation stage so the proposed configuration can be reviewed.

Frequently asked questions

Does a sticking door mean the leaf is warped?

Not necessarily. Frame alignment, hardware position, floor changes, finishes and the leaf itself all need consideration. Establish the contact point and inspect the complete opening before assigning a cause.

Can every interior door be trimmed to stop rubbing?

No universal trimming allowance applies. Obtain instructions for the actual door and review any warranty, construction or certification constraints before modifying it.

What is the most useful first step on a multi-room project?

Document representative affected and unaffected openings using the same record format. This gives the project team a comparison before it approves a repeated repair.
