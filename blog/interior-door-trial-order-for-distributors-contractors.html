VISFURN · Samples & Trial Orders

Interior door samples and trial orders for distributors and contractors

Plan an interior-door sample or commercial trial: buyer role, evaluation purpose, configuration, MOQ, packing, freight and the next sourcing decision.

For importers, distributors and project buyersUpdated 7 November 2026

Define what the first order needs to prove

A distributor sample, a working opening and a commercial trial have different purposes. A small request can support a serious sourcing decision, but the buyer’s role, application, approval process and next step need to be clear. Start with the decision you need to make, then agree the smallest useful sample or trial scope that is feasible for the selected model.

This guide covers commercial planning. For detailed construction, dimension, finish and hardware checks, use the technical sample-approval checklist.

Choose the right type of first request

Match the request to a decision
RequestUseful forWhat it does not establish alone

Cross-section or component sampleReviewing leaf construction, edge or frame profileAll details of a full working door or production batch
Working leaf and frameFit, handing, hardware preparation and installation coordinationA universal size range or approval for every project opening
Commercial trial batchTesting an agreed range, receiving process, packaging and installation workflowGuaranteed resale, future volume or automatic repeat-order pricing

Tell us whether you need a loose sample, a functioning opening or a saleable first batch. Availability, quantity, configuration, price, packing and freight must be confirmed before acceptance.

What a distributor should put in the inquiry

Describe your sales channel and the intended market: importer stock, dealer network, showroom or project supply. Give the door types you currently sell, the size range you want to evaluate and whether you handle finishing, frame assembly or installation locally. A website or company introduction can help explain the channel, but an accurate product requirement matters more than a long company presentation.

  • Range: preferred design, construction direction, sizes and estimated quantities by model.
  • Finish route: primed for local finishing, final white finish, woodgrain film or another defined surface.
  • Scope: leaf only, leaf plus frame and trim, or specified hardware. Identify local components that need to match.
  • Evaluation: who will inspect the goods and what acceptance criteria they will use.
  • Next decision: planned review date and what would justify a batch quotation. An estimate is sufficient; do not present an unapproved forecast as a firm order.

Use the white primed versus white finished comparison when local finishing is part of your business model. Request feasible configurations for the selected model rather than assuming a photographed finished door can be supplied in any substrate or primer system.

What a contractor should put in the inquiry

Identify the project type, destination and the specific opening or room group being evaluated. Attach the schedule or drawing revision that the trial relates to. If existing frames will remain, provide their dimensions, profiles, hinge and strike details. If a new frame is required, give the finished wall thickness and the dimensional basis for the opening.

A working sample is most useful when the intended installer or technical approver can assess it. Agree who checks the fit, handing, hardware templates, finish, protection and installation sequence. Keep performance-document review separate and tied to the project’s actual requirements; a satisfactory visual mock-up is not a fire or acoustic approval.

Where a project has many variants, select a representative configuration and list the variants it does not cover. One room mock-up should not silently approve different leaf sizes, hardware sets, glazed openings or exposure conditions.

Keep standard batch terms separate from sample review

For standard wooden, WPC and PVC batch orders, the minimum is 50 sets total, with at least 10 sets per model. Mixing models does not remove the per-model minimum. Send the proposed breakdown so feasibility can be reviewed against the actual configuration.

A professional sample or smaller commercial trial can be discussed separately. This is a review route, not a published lower MOQ or an automatic exception. State what you want to evaluate and the quantity you propose; obtain written confirmation of acceptance, price, freight and timing before making project commitments.

Do not assume a free sample, a sample-charge refund, future order credit, exclusive territory or reserved production capacity. If any commercial arrangement is offered, record its amount, conditions and validity in the quotation.

Compare the complete trial cost and responsibilities

Ask for identifiable cost lines for goods or samples, any agreed preparation or tooling, export packing and the quoted transport service. Request the packed dimensions and weight for the proposed configuration. Freight for a small consignment should be assessed from the actual packing, route and service scope rather than extrapolated from a container quote.

Identify the delivery term and named place or port. Confirm who handles import clearance, applicable taxes, unloading and local delivery. A sample courier service and a bulk sea-freight shipment may have different responsibility boundaries. Ask the forwarder or relevant service provider for the current route-specific charges.

Before comparing a knock-down package with a prehung assembly, include the local assembly and handling plan. A lower goods price may be accompanied by a different scope of work. The supply-package guide helps make those differences explicit.

Set a practical approval and follow-up plan

  1. Before quoting: agree the purpose, selected model, required parts, dimensions, finish state and destination.
  2. Before making the sample or trial: confirm the drawing or specification revision, commercial terms and approval responsibilities.
  3. Before dispatch: review the agreed inspection evidence, identification and packing. Confirm which items are still awaiting approval.
  4. After receipt: record unpacking condition, component completeness and the specific fit or installation observations. Link photographs to the sample or opening ID.
  5. Before a repeat quotation: confirm corrections, approved configuration, quantities by size/model and target schedule. Update the revision instead of relying on chat memory.

Keep an approval log with the sample ID, model, drawing revision, approver, review date and outcome. Use clear outcomes such as approved, approved with listed conditions, or revision required. A positive comment on appearance should not become blanket approval of dimensions and hardware.

Send a concise professional sample or trial brief

You can copy these points into your inquiry:

  • Company / buyer role / destination country and port.
  • Purpose: material review, showroom display, working opening or commercial trial.
  • Preferred product link, quantities by model and size, and intended room use.
  • Leaf/frame/trim/hardware scope; knock-down or prehung requirement if known.
  • Finish state and reference, including whether local painting is planned.
  • Approver, evaluation criteria, requested timing and next sourcing decision.

Start with the interior door collection, wooden doors or WPC doors. The existing door-schedule template (CSV) can accompany your own project drawings. Unknown details can be flagged for review rather than guessed.
