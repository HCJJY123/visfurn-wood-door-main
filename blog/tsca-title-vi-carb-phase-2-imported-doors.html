TSCA Title VI and CARB Phase 2 for Imported Doors: The Importer’s Compliance Checklist

A buyer-focused checklist for MDF, HDF and plywood doors entering North America, including TPC certification, labels and importer records.

United States / CanadaPrepared for B2B door buyers

Direct Answer

Doors containing MDF, HDF, particleboard or hardwood plywood may be regulated composite wood products. Importers should verify the applicable emission documentation, EPA-recognised TPC certification, label details and ACE filing requirements for the actual components in the door set.

Which doors are regulated under TSCA Title VI?

What should the label on an imported door show?

The label should identify the fabricator, production month and year, and the applicable TSCA Title VI statement. Use the actual approved label format; do not rely on a generic artwork file or a supplier screenshot.

What is the difference between TSCA Title VI and CARB Phase 2?

The standards are closely aligned for regulated composite wood products, but the accepted certification pathway and importer records still matter. A buyer should request the current EPA-recognised TPC information rather than assuming that a historic CARB document is sufficient for every US entry.

What records should an importer keep?

Keep supplier declarations, TPC information, production records, labels, test reports and purchase documents in a retrievable project file. If an authority requests records, the importer must be able to provide the relevant evidence within the required response period.

How do you verify a supplier certificate?

  • Request the TPC name and certificate reference
  • Cross-check the certifier against the recognised list
  • Match the certified panel, thickness and resin system to the door BOM
  • Review recent test reports and factory inspection records
  • Use independent sampling when the project or market risk justifies it

FAQ

Questions buyers usually ask

Are solid wood doors exempt from TSCA Title VI?

They may be exempt when they contain no regulated composite wood component. Confirm the actual bill of materials.

Does a WPC door need CARB certification?

Usually not when it contains no regulated composite wood, but the frame or trim may introduce MDF or plywood components.

Can a Chinese factory self-certify?

The importer should verify certification through an EPA-recognised third-party certifier rather than accepting an unverified self-issued claim.

Is an NAF resin door still labelled?

Follow the applicable label and record requirements for the certified product and resin system.

Who files the TSCA import certification?

The importer of record normally handles the import certification through the required customs process.
