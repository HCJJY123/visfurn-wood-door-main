Door Submittal Package Checklist for Project Approval

A coordination guide for contractors, developers, importers and consultants reviewing door schedules, shop drawings, finishes and hardware before repeated production.

A useful door submittal package is one coordinated approval record, not a folder of unrelated PDFs. It should connect every opening mark to the door and frame configuration, hardware preparation, finish reference, responsibility boundary and current revision. Production should be released only under the written status and conditions defined by the project team.

What is a door submittal package?

A door submittal package is the set of schedules, drawings, product information and approval records used to check what will be supplied for specified openings. It sits between the initial quotation and production. The project specification, architect or consultant determines the required documents and approval terminology; there is no universal package that fits every project.

This guide applies to bulk interior, hotel, apartment, hospital and related project-door procurement. It is not an approval service, a certification claim or a substitute for the responsible designer. Use the door schedule quotation guide when the project is still at RFQ stage, and the door sample approval checklist when a physical sample or installed pilot opening is required.

1. Start with a controlled transmittal and opening register

Give the package a project name, package ID, revision, issue date and stated purpose. List the openings or door types covered, the files included and the status requested. A door mark such as D-101 should mean the same opening in the architectural schedule, door drawing, frame drawing and hardware schedule.

Do not hide missing information. Mark it as an open item, name the responsible party and state whether it prevents review or production. If one part of the package changes, identify the affected opening marks instead of silently replacing a file.

2. Coordinate the schedule, drawings and product scope

The door schedule is the index. Each unique type should lead to drawings or written details that explain the leaf, frame, trim, glazing, finish and preparation. Confirm dimensions in the same measurement basis: rough opening, finished opening, leaf size, frame outside size and wall or jamb depth are different values.

  • Door and frame: material or construction route, dimensions, thickness, handing, swing, frame profile, jamb depth, trim and threshold interface.
  • Finish: face and edge finish, frame and trim finish, colour or approved sample reference, and areas where finishes intentionally differ.
  • Glazing or ventilation: opening size and position, visible layout and the party responsible for glass, bead, grille or louvre components.
  • Performance requirements: the exact opening requirement and the documents requested by the project. Do not substitute a generic certificate or statement for configuration-specific review.
  • Supply boundary: whether the quotation includes leaf only, leaf and frame, architraves, seals, threshold, hardware, packing labels or other items.

University of Maryland project standards provide one real example of coordinated review: their door section calls for related door, frame and hardware schedules and product data to be submitted together. That is a project-specific requirement, not a rule that VISFURN applies to every order.

3. Lock down hardware preparation before fabrication

Hardware is not only a purchasing list. It can determine hinge locations, lock case cut-outs, reinforcement, strike preparation, closer fixing, electrical pathways and frame details. Coordinate hardware by opening mark and hardware set, then supply the manufacturer's current templates where machining depends on them.

Use the hardware preparation checklist for a more detailed model and template review. Cornell University's door-hardware standard is another project-specific example: it requires a hardware schedule coordinated with doors and frames, cross-referenced to openings, and supported by product data, samples and affected shop drawings. It also calls for early submission where approval must precede fabrication.

4. Record finish and sample decisions without over-claiming approval

Name the reference being reviewed: digital colour indication, material swatch, door-face sample, full-size sample door or installed mock-up. These are not interchangeable. Record the sample ID, date, reviewer response, comments and which door types it controls. If the approval is conditional, repeat the condition in the production-release record.

A finish sample does not establish dimensions, hardware operation or the suitability of an assembly for a regulated opening. Conversely, an approved drawing does not prove that a colour or surface texture has been accepted. Keep the two decisions connected but explicit.

5. Use a responsibility matrix to prevent scope gaps

Door packages often interface with the main contractor, installer, hardware supplier, access-control contractor and local consultant. Assign one owner for each input and deliverable before the submittal deadline.

6. Close comments and control revisions before production release

Return comments against the same opening marks and drawing numbers used in the package. A response such as “updated” is difficult to verify; state what changed and where. Keep a comment-response log, mark superseded files, and issue a complete coordinated revision rather than relying on several email attachments.

Review labels differ between projects. “Approved,” “approved as noted,” “reviewed,” “revise and resubmit” or other wording may have different contractual meaning. Use the project's defined terminology. The buyer and responsible project team should confirm in writing whether the response releases production, releases only selected door types, or holds the order pending remaining information.

Production-release check

  1. Does the current package identify every included opening and revision?
  2. Do schedule, door drawings, frame details and hardware sets agree?
  3. Are buyer-supplied hardware templates and preparation details complete?
  4. Are finish or mock-up responses recorded against the correct door types?
  5. Are performance requirements and requested documents tied to the proposed configuration?
  6. Are supply, installation and commissioning boundaries explicit?
  7. Are all review comments closed or clearly accepted as conditions?
  8. Has an authorised party issued a written release for the intended production scope?

What to send for a VISFURN project review

For an initial commercial and configuration review, send the door schedule or opening list, relevant plans or elevations, quantities, rough openings and leaf sizes, finished wall thickness, frame and trim scope, hardware models or templates, finish reference, destination and required project dates. Identify hotel, hospital, apartment or other application areas and list any project standards or documents that the responsible consultant requires.

VISFURN can review the requested door configuration and state the available supply scope in a quotation. The review does not replace local design approval, site measurement, authority acceptance or installation responsibility. For application routes, compare hotel door packages and hospital door configurations.

Buyer questions

What should be included in a door submittal package?

The exact requirements come from the project documents. A coordinated package commonly connects the door schedule, door and frame drawings, hardware schedule and preparation, finish references, applicable product data, scope responsibilities and a revision record.

Does an approved shop drawing automatically release doors for production?

Not necessarily. The project team should define whether the response authorises production and whether conditions, open comments, finish approval, hardware information or other related submissions remain outstanding.

Can final hardware information be provided after the doors are ordered?

Only if the affected preparation is intentionally held and the commercial and schedule consequences are accepted. Hardware model, backset, handing, mounting and template information may affect the leaf and frame before production.

How should door submittal revisions be controlled?

Use a package ID and revision, identify changed opening marks, keep a comment-response log, mark superseded files and obtain a written status for the complete coordinated package before production release.

Prepare a coordinated project RFQ

Send the schedule, drawings and door-package requirements together.

Include quantities, opening sizes, wall thickness, frame and hardware scope, finish references, destination, project dates and the standards or approval documents requested by your consultant. VISFURN will review the requested configuration and confirm what can be quoted.
